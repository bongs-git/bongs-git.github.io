# Keeping the INDAI website in step with the Quest app

The website (this folder, served at https://bongs-git.github.io/indai/) and the Meta Quest command center
([bongs-git/jarvis-command-center](https://github.com/bongs-git/jarvis-command-center)) are one product with two faces.
They share the same backend and the same brains. Only the presentation layer differs.

Synced at commit: `17629cb16fd451d03135d991e0c53b2a68de0be6` (2026-11-19)

## When a feature lands in either app

1. Add a row to `FEATURES.md` in jarvis-command-center (the `web` column says whether the website has it).
2. If it changed a shared file, run `./sync.sh` here and fix anything it flags.
3. If it changed `main.js`, `panels.js` or `ui.js`, port the matching part to `app.js`, `views.js` or `sheets.js`.
4. Test: `python3 -m http.server` in the repo root, open `/indai/?demo=1`, try the phone, tablet and laptop widths.

## Small differences on purpose

- Typed commands do not open the microphone by themselves; tapping the mic brings hands-free follow-ups back.
- A typed command during the morning check-in runs as a command instead of being taken as the answer.
- In demo mode the option chain is simulated from the demo prices (the Quest app shows a "couldn't load the chain" message offline).
- If the app fell back to demo prices because the network was down, it retries and switches back to real data by itself. The fail-fast switch in `demo.js` only applies while on demo prices; on real data every request goes out.
- The automatic morning check-in waits until the mic is closed and nothing is open before she starts asking.
- Undo also takes back a cooldown that the undone loss started; a calculator opened for a ticker with no price yet snaps its strike to the chain.
- Levels in demo mode are always the simulated ones, so they match the simulated prices.
- The service worker also keeps the voice runtime (`voice-worker.js`, `vendor/ort`) in a cache of its own. `sync.sh` renames that cache when those files change.
- The chart and lead/lag fit the session so far (with room to the right) instead of always drawing the full 9:30 to 4:00 width, and chart labels move aside rather than overlap.
- Coming back to the app, or getting signal back, pulls fresh prices at once; a failed quote pull is retried within seconds.
- Trade ideas (`ideas.js`, `app.js`): the setup scanner and its rules are the shared `signals.js`, unchanged. What is different is everything around it. The scan runs the moment new candles land (and every 5 seconds as a backstop) instead of every 15 seconds, and bridge candles are looked for every 2.5 seconds after the minute turns. The day's ideas are kept for the New York day (`localStorage` key `indai.web.ideas.v1`), so a reload never loses them, and setups that confirmed while the page was closed or asleep are replayed from today's candles and listed quietly ("Earlier today"). A setup past the plan's alerts-per-hour limit is listed without an alert instead of being dropped. A setup taking shape on the candle that is still open shows as "Forming" from its second minute (volume projected to the full candle) and confirms or disappears at the close. Out loud she says a short version (ticker, direction, grade, contract, stop, target); the full text is in the conversation. Each idea shows what price did next (target reached, stopped, or still open), and the chart marks every idea with an arrow and draws stop and target for the one in focus. "Skip" on the pinned card only takes it off that card.
- Retrospective (`retro.js`, button in the Ideas list, chip in the strip after the close, "retrospective" typed or spoken): the scanner itself (`signals.js`) is untouched. The website replays every setup the scanner found that day at the plan's score level from the day's candles, measures each against its stop and target on the one-minute candles (R multiples), and keeps finished days on the device (`localStorage` key `indai.web.retro.v1`, last 20 trading days, real data only). From all kept days it proposes changes: points up or down for a time window, kind of setup, direction, ticker or warning (at least 8 setups in the group, 20 overall, pulled toward the average, capped at −10 and +3, total −15 to +8), a higher score line (one 3-point step at a time, only when what it cuts was losing and at least half remain), and a different stop or target distance (30 setups, clearly and consistently better across days). Nothing changes until "Apply"; applied changes go onto each fresh setup in `app.tune` before the score line, with the scanner's own numbers kept on the idea; "Clear what she has learned" undoes it all. The lesson is also saved to backend memory (`kind: rule`). The backend's push scanner does not use these adjustments.
- Back-test (`retro.js`, shown inside the Retrospective): `backtest` runs the kept days with and without a set of changes (which setups would still be alerted, measured with the proposed stop and target distance) and `metrics` gives setups, wins and losses, win rate, result in R, average per setup, profit factor, deepest drawdown and longest losing run, overall and per day; dollars use the plan's risk per trade. Those numbers are in-sample: the changes were learned from the same days. `walkForward` is the fair test: each day is judged with only what `propose` would have suggested from the days before it, and it needs at least two kept days with enough setups before it shows anything.
- TradingView (`tradingview/indai-backtest.pine`): the 14 setups, the scoring, the 20-minute repeat rule and the alerts-per-hour limit of `signals.js`, written for a 5-minute regular-hours chart, with the indicators reset each session the way the app computes them. A JavaScript copy of the same bar-by-bar logic was checked against `scanSetups` on 4,680 candles and matched on every one; the Pine file itself has not been compiled outside TradingView. Differences from the app: it takes one trade at a time (a new alert while in a trade is marked but not traded), exits at the stop, the target or the last candle of the session, sizes by dollars risked per trade in whole shares, and knows GEX walls only if they are typed into its settings. The Retrospective's "Copy the changes for TradingView" gives `minadd=…;stop=…;target=…;time:…=…` to paste into the strategy's "Changes" setting; a unit test checks that the flag names and setup names in the .pine file match the app's.
- Chart moving, zooming and drawing (`chart.js`, `draw.js`, website only): dragging sideways moves the chart, two fingers or a trackpad pinch zoom it (a plain wheel zooms where the page itself cannot scroll), two quick taps or "Reset view" go back to the automatic view, and the view also resets when the ticker, candle size or session changes. The price scale always fits the candles on screen. "Draw" opens a bar with Trend line (drag, or tap the start and then the end; it is carried on to the right edge as a dashed line) and Level (tap a price). A drawing is tapped to select it, then dragged to move it or, for a line, dragged by an end; Delete, Clear all, the Delete key and Escape work on the selection. While the bar is open or something is selected the chart takes all touch movement (`touch-action: none`); otherwise up-and-down swipes still scroll the page. Points are stored as time and price, so they hold their place across candle sizes; levels stay until deleted, trend lines belong to the session they were drawn on and are dropped after a week. Drawings are on the `over` canvas, so drawing and the crosshair never redraw the candles. `app.chart` is the chart instance (used by the tests).
- Chart axes, earlier sessions and the TradingView panel (`chart.js`, `history.js`, `views.js`, website only): the price axis and the time axis are drag handles (two strips over the canvas with `touch-action: none`); dragging the price axis sets the price range by hand (`chart.ys`), after which the chart also moves up and down, and two quick taps on an axis hand it back to the automatic fit. Earlier sessions sit at negative slots to the left of today's open, packed without overnight gaps (`slotOf` / `timeOf` in `chart.js` turn a time into a slot and back, which is what keeps drawings in place across days); they are fetched when the view comes within 60 slots of the oldest loaded candle, a day line with its date marks where each session starts, and zoom-out is capped at 2,400 slots. The history only holds what the bridge has stored since November 19, 2026. "TradingView chart" swaps the canvases for TradingView's embedded chart (`https://s.tradingview.com/widgetembed/…` in an `<iframe credentialless>`, needed because the page is cross-origin isolated for the voice models; no TradingView script runs in this origin) for the same ticker and candle size; `app.showTradingView(on)` toggles it.
- Extended hours (`ext.js`, `history.js`, `chart.js`, website only): the shared `feeds.js` only hands the chart regular-hours candles (`getBars`), so the website keeps its own store of the candles outside them. After the close they follow the session one slot each (`geo.X`, `geo.xb`; the overnight gap is not drawn), and `history.series()` weaves earlier ones between earlier sessions with `x` marking them; VWAP and the EMAs stay the regular session's, so those candles carry none. `settings.show.ext` (the "Ext" switch, on unless turned off) hides them. The line under the price is always there so the layout never jumps: the extended-hours price and its change from the regular close, or the day's high and low (the one-line high and low, `#ch-hl`, is no longer shown). In demo mode the candles after the demo session are made up. Not in the Quest app.
- Bridge feed (`app.js` `pollBridge`, `bridgeGap`, website only): the answer to `GET indai-tv?bars=1&since=…` carries a `cursor`; the website sends it back as `seen` (appended to the `since` it hands the shared `relay.tvBars`), so each answer holds only what was stored since, and "something new arrived" is known exactly. With updates of the forming candle arriving (about every 15 seconds), it rests 12.5 seconds after one, then looks every 1.5 seconds until the next is in; with finished candles only it looks every 2.5 seconds from just after the minute turns until the candle is in, and every 8 seconds otherwise. Not in the Quest app.
- Access lock (`lock.js`): the backend's `INDAI_PASSCODE` is the lock. When `GET indai-brain` reports `passcode: true`, a device without an accepted token shows only the lock screen: no prices, alerts or voice start behind it. The token is checked by the backend (`mode: memory, op: list` with `x-indai-pass`), kept in `settings.researchPass` (the same field the Quest app uses), and asked for again if the backend stops accepting it. Settings can lock the device again. The market-data, TradingView read and GEX endpoints are not behind the passcode on the backend, and the site's files are public.
- Push (`push.js`, `sw.js`): Settings has "Turn on push notifications" (permission, `GET indai-push` for the key, `pushManager.subscribe`, `POST indai-push {op: subscribe}`), a test and "Turn off". While it is on, `mode: profile, op: put` keeps `plan` (date, aggr, capital, goal, strategies, pnl, goalHit, tripped, cooldownUntil, symbols, manual levels) and `watchlist` current within seconds of a change. The service worker shows each push (quietly if INDAI is on screen) and a tap opens that ticker in Ideas. iPhone and iPad need the Home Screen app.
- Thinking budget care (`demo.js`): when the backend answers `429 budget` (or `rate_limited`), she says once in plain words that today's thinking budget is used up until midnight New York time, then keeps working with built-in commands and market-data ratings, with no "couldn't reach my thinking brain" plus "I heard …" pair. She checks again every five minutes, or at once from Settings. Web research that fails twice in a row on the backend (`502 parse` / `upstream`, or a timeout) pauses itself until midnight New York time, because each failed try still spends the budget; Settings has "Turn web research back on".
- Being heard on a phone (`app.js`, no change to `voice.js`): on iPhone and iPad the audio session is set to `playback` so the ring/silent switch does not mute her (a looping silent clip does the same on older iOS), and to `play-and-record` only while the mic is open. The mic is released the moment listening ends instead of 25 seconds later, because iOS plays everything at quiet call volume while it is open. The audio engine is woken on every tap and on coming back to the app (`interrupted`, or `running` with a stalled clock). The phone's own voice is unlocked by the first tap. If the natural voice takes more than about 3.5 seconds to start talking twice in a row on a phone, she uses the phone's own voice for the rest of the visit and says so. Settings shows a Sound line with all of this.
- Safari and iOS always listen with the on-device model on WebAssembly (no browser speech recognition, no WebGPU), unless "Browser speech recognition" is picked in Settings.

## Website final touches — November 20, 2026

Website-only adapters preserve the shared files above. `data-status.js` retains relay source/delay metadata for
per-ticker price badges, tap explanations and chart labels. A bridge candle is called live only while it is under
two minutes old. Unknown previous close is shown as an em dash; failed option chains are explained in the
calculator, verdict, levels and idea contract card.

`draw.js` now supports undo, horizontal rays, rectangles and cancelled-gesture rollback. `chart.js` adds a
long-press magnifier and earlier-session time labels. History loading/end messages do not reset a scrolled view.
TradingView uses its vendor-hosted Advanced Chart inside a credentialless, cross-origin sandboxed iframe;
third-party code never executes in the app's origin. The new Web Component was tested in an opaque srcdoc
frame but failed on cookie access, so it is not shipped. The hosted embed can use its own origin's storage. Full screen has a CSS fallback and an external chart link.
The vendor-hosted chart rendered with candles and controls in the desktop preview. Actual iPhone/iPad
rendering and iPhone silent-ring speech/closed-app push remain device release checks.

`profile-sync.js` serializes local changes and retains pending work across visits. Drawing profiles use
`{version:1, items:{[id]:drawingOrTombstone}}` with per-item `symbol` and `updatedAt` milliseconds.
Tuning uses `{version:1, adj, minAdd, stop, target, at, basis}`. The current backend rejects both profile keys
(`drawings`, `tuning`); Settings says that work is saved locally pending backend support. Do not claim cloud
sync until the backend accepts them. Drawings require an atomic per-ID merge (latest timestamp, tombstone
wins ties), not whole-profile replacement. Tuning requires latest-`at` selection, including reset records.
These backend requests belong to Ikpa. Schwab order submission is built: the back end is `indai-orders` (its contract is in the app repository's `HANDOFF.md`) and the page is `trade.html`.

Push receipts persist locally in a separate Cache API cache. Delay is measured from the completed five-minute
candle's close (`barT + 300000`) to service-worker receipt, not notification click; test notifications do not
overwrite the last market alert. Settings also reports the test endpoint's accepted-device count.
